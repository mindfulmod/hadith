‘Abdullah ibn Muhammad related to us. He said: Sufyan related to us. He said: ‘Amr related to us. He said: Sa‘id ibn Jubayr informed me. He said:

I said to Ibn ‘Abbas: “Nawf al-Bakali claims that Musa was not the Musa of the Children of Israel, but another Musa.” He said: “The enemy of Allah has lied.”

Ubayy ibn Ka‘b related to us, from the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace:

“Musa the prophet stood to address the Children of Israel. He was asked: ‘Which person has the most knowledge?’ He said: ‘I have the most knowledge.’ Allah reproached him because he had not referred knowledge to Him. Allah revealed to him: ‘A servant of Mine at the meeting of the two seas has more knowledge than you.’ He said: ‘My Lord, how can I reach him?’ He was told: ‘Carry a fish in a basket. When you lose it, he will be there.’

“He set out with his young companion, Yusha‘ ibn Nun, carrying a fish in a basket. When they reached the rock, they laid down their heads and slept. The fish slipped out of the basket and ‘took its way into the sea as through a tunnel.’ It was a wonder to Musa and his young companion. They travelled on for the rest of their night and day. When morning came, Musa said to his young companion: ‘Bring us our meal; we have suffered weariness from this journey of ours.’ Musa had felt no weariness until he passed the place to which he had been directed. His young companion said to him: ‘Do you remember when we sheltered at the rock? I forgot the fish.’ Musa said: ‘That is what we were seeking.’ ‘So they returned, retracing their footsteps.’

“When they reached the rock, there was a man covered with a garment—or he said: he had covered himself with his garment. Musa greeted him. Al-Khadir said: ‘How is there this greeting in your land?’ He said: ‘I am Musa.’ He said: ‘Musa of the Children of Israel?’ He said: ‘Yes.’ He said: ‘May I follow you so that you teach me some of the right guidance you have been taught?’ He said: ‘You will not be able to be patient with me. Musa, I have knowledge from Allah’s knowledge which He taught me and which you do not know, and you have knowledge He taught you which I do not know.’ ‘He said: You will find me patient, if Allah wills, and I will not disobey you in any matter.’

“They set off walking along the seashore, without a boat. A boat passed them, and they spoke to its people to take them aboard. Al-Khadir was recognised, and they carried them without a fare. A sparrow came and settled on the edge of the boat, dipping its beak once or twice into the sea. Al-Khadir said: ‘Musa, my knowledge and your knowledge have diminished Allah’s knowledge only as this sparrow’s dipping has diminished the sea.’

“Al-Khadir went to one of the boat’s planks and pulled it out. Musa said: ‘People carried us without a fare, and you have gone to their boat and made a hole in it to drown its people!’ ‘He said: Did I not say that you would not be able to be patient with me?’ ‘He said: Do not take me to task for what I forgot.’ Musa’s first lapse was forgetfulness.

“They went on, and there was a boy playing with other boys. Al-Khadir took hold of the top of his head and pulled his head off with his hand. Musa said: ‘Have you killed an innocent soul, without its having killed a soul?’ ‘He said: Did I not say to you that you would not be able to be patient with me?’”

Ibn ‘Uyaynah said: “This is more emphatic.”

“They went on until, when they came to the people of a village, they asked its people for food, but they refused to give them hospitality. They found there a wall about to collapse, and he set it upright.” Al-Khadir gestured with his hand and set it upright. Musa said to him: “Had you wished, you could have taken payment for it.” “He said: This is the parting between me and you.”

The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said: “May Allah have mercy on Musa. We would have wished that he had been patient, so that more of their affair would have been related to us.”
