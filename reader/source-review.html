SAHIH AL-BUKHARI · 1

Opening narration 1

Working English translation · AI cross-check completed · No independent scholarly certification

English

Al-Humaydi, ‘Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, related to us: Sufyan related to us: Yahya ibn Sa‘id al-Ansari related to us: Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Taymi informed me that he heard ‘Alqamah ibn Waqqas al-Laythi say: I heard ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah Most High be pleased with him, on the pulpit. He said:

I heard the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, say: “Actions are only according to intentions, and each person will have only what they intended. Whoever’s migration is for worldly gain or to marry a woman, their migration is to that for which they migrated.”

العربية

[1] حدثنا الحميدي عبد الله بن الزبير قال: حدثنا سفيان قال: حدثنا يحيى بن سعيد الأنصارى قال: أخبرني محمد بن إبراهيم التيمي أنه سمع علقمة بن وقاص الليثي يقول: سمعت عمر بن الخطاب رضى الله تعالى عنه على المنبر قال:

سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: إنما الأعمال بالنيات وإنما لكل امرئ ما نوى فمن كانت هجرته إلى دنيا يصيبها أو إلى امرأة ينكحها فهجرته إلى ما هاجر إليه.

Translation decisions & comparison
Arabic clause / checkDecision and evidence
إنما الأعمال بالنياتPreserve the restrictive force of إنما. Use a close English rendering; do not insert the explicit noun “reward”, which appears in Khan’s explanatory rendering but not in this Arabic clause.

Khan and Bewley both relate the valuation of deeds to intentions. Their English wording differs.

وإنما لكل امرئ ما نوىPreserve each person and the intention-specific result. Gender-neutral English does not narrow the generic Arabic person to men.

Both English comparators preserve individual intention.

إلى دنيا يصيبها أو إلى امرأة ينكحهاKeep both alternatives: obtaining worldly benefit and marrying a woman.

Both comparators include both alternatives.

فهجرته إلى ما هاجر إليهPreserve the conclusion without adding a separate moral explanation.

Both comparators retain this conclusion.

على المنبرRetain the pulpit setting in the full translated narrator introduction. Khan’s displayed abridged introduction does not include the full chain; Bewley includes the pulpit setting.

Checked against the Arabic witnesses.

Variant boundaryDo not import the additional migration-to-Allah-and-His-Messenger clause from other transmissions of this hadith. It is absent from the selected opening entry in all Arabic witnesses inspected.

Opening entries in Khan and Bewley also follow the shorter form.

SAHIH AL-BUKHARI · 2

Opening narration 2

Working English translation · AI cross-check completed · No independent scholarly certification

English

‘Abdullah ibn Yusuf related to us: Malik informed us, from Hisham ibn ‘Urwah, from his father, from ‘A’ishah, the Mother of the Believers, may Allah Most High be pleased with her, that al-Harith ibn Hisham, may Allah Most High be pleased with him, asked the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace:

He said, “Messenger of Allah, how does revelation come to you?” The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said: “At times it comes to me like the ringing of a bell, and this is the hardest form for me. It then leaves me, and I have understood what he said. At other times the angel appears to me as a man and speaks to me, and I understand what he says.” ‘A’ishah, may Allah Most High be pleased with her, said: “I saw revelation descend upon him on a bitterly cold day. It then left him, while his forehead was streaming with sweat.”

العربية

[2] حدثنا عبد الله بن يوسف قال: أخبرنا مالك عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه عن عائشة أم المؤمنين رضى الله تعالى عنها أن الحارث بن هشام رضى الله تعالى عنه سأل رسول الله ﷺ،

فقال: يا رسول الله كيف يأتيك الوحي، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: أحيانا يأتيني مثل صلصلة الجرس وهو أشده علي فيفصم عني وقد وعيت عنه ما قال وأحيانا يتمثل لي الملك رجلا فيكلمني فأعي ما يقول: قالت عائشة رضى الله تعالى عنها ولقد رأيته ينزل عليه الوحي في اليوم الشديد البرد فيفصم عنه وإن جبينه ليتفصد عرقا.

Translation decisions & comparison
Arabic clause / checkDecision and evidence
كيف يأتيك الوحيTranslate الوحي consistently as revelation. Preserve al-Harith’s question as a question.

Bewley uses revelation; Sunnah.com’s Khan rendering uses Divine Inspiration, while the Darussalam sample uses Divine Revelation. Same underlying concept; not three independent translators.

مثل صلصلة الجرسPreserve the comparison to a ringing bell; do not state that a physical bell was present.

Both English translators preserve a comparison.

وهو أشده عليKeep the superlative and its relation to the speaker; do not weaken it to merely difficult.

Both translators preserve the comparative severity.

فيفصم عني وقد وعيت عنه ما قالKeep the sequence: it leaves, and the message has been understood. The Arabic ما قال refers to what he said; retain the pronoun without inserting Gabriel’s name. وعيت includes understanding/retention; the draft uses understood rather than adding a separate interpretive sentence.

Bewley makes understanding and retention explicit; Khan describes grasping the revealed content.

يتمثل لي الملك رجلاKeep the angel appearing in a man’s form. Do not imply an ordinary man authored the revelation.

Both translators preserve the angel and the human form.

فيكلمني فأعي ما يقولPreserve speech to the Prophet and his understanding of that speech.

Both English comparators retain both actions.

قالت عائشةEnd the Prophet’s quoted answer before ‘A’ishah’s observation; do not merge the two speakers.

Both comparators distinguish the speakers.

في اليوم الشديد البرد … وإن جبينه ليتفصد عرقاPreserve both severe cold and sweat flowing from the forehead. Do not replace these details with a generalized description.

Both English comparators retain the weather and forehead/sweat details.

What the comparison established

  • The selected digital Arabic includes full chains of transmission; the working English retains them.
  • The Brill print abbreviates the first narrator to al-Humaydi while the selected digital text and Darussalam give his name. Honorific wording and orthography also differ. These are recorded as witness differences, not silently harmonized.
  • The first two narration bodies were visually compared against the Darussalam Arabic and the older print. No additional substantive body clause was identified in those comparisons. This is an AI review, not an independent human audit.
  • The sample and a digital edition are not presumed identical merely because their translator is the same.
  • A close translation still involves linguistic judgment; zero interpretation and infallibility cannot honestly be promised.
