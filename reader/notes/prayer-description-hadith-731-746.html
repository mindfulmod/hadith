Comparison findings

  • The forgetting remark remains separate from the prayer description; Ibn Hajar attributes it to Sayyar. The selected did not mind is retained rather than substituted with the preference wording in Bewley. The two rakahs or one uncertainty and sixty to a hundred range remain; the source has no explicit noun verses after the count.

AI-assisted working translation of the complete selected Arabic, including the full chain, repeated statements, uncertainty and attributed comments. Two visually inspected Arabic prints and two individually matched English comparisons were consulted. The independently transcribed report has its own print origin. Complete working English

Adam related to us. He said: Shu‘bah related to us. He said: Sayyar ibn Salamah related to us. He said:

My father and I visited Abu Barzah al-Aslami and asked him about the times of the prayers. He said: The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to pray the noon prayer when the sun declined, and the afternoon prayer when a man could return to the furthest part of Madinah while the sun was still bright.

I have forgotten what he said about the sunset prayer.

He did not mind delaying the night prayer until a third of the night. He did not like sleeping before it or conversation afterwards. He would pray the dawn prayer and a man would leave able to recognize the person sitting beside him. He used to recite between sixty and a hundred in the two rak‘ahs—or in one of them.

Complete working English

Musaddad related to us. He said: Isma‘il ibn Ibrahim related to us. He said: Ibn Jurayj informed us. He said: ‘Ata’ informed me that he heard Abu Hurayrah, may Allah Most High be pleased with him, say:
