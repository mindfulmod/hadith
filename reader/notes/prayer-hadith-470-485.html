Comparison findings

  • This one selected entry contains nine accounts of prayer stops. Both printed witnesses were inspected across their complete spans; the modern print numbers the accounts 484–492. All repeated attributions, location clauses and thirteen Prophet honorifics remain. The English is a new provisional translation, not copied from the comparison.
  • The pinned Khan-family reference 1.8.471 contains only an omission notice saying that the locations are impossible to translate; it supplies no English report body. Bewley 470 provides a complete English comparison, read in full. This entry therefore has only one complete English comparison, alongside two Arabic prints and the selected digital Arabic.
  • Ibn Hajar’s lexical discussion was consulted for marks out, al-‘Irq, the road features, place names and measures. Samurah and sarhah remain distinct transliterated tree terms. The selected until is retained although the historical print has when. Miles and cubits are not converted into modern measures.
  • The sarhah’s broken top, trunk and sand heaps remain. Dorar’s explanation explicitly identifies the tree as the antecedent. The road’s salimat is provisionally rendered rocks; Ibn Hajar records a different vocalization denoting trees. The final repeated mosque wording and changes of grammatical person are preserved without silently repairing the geography. Independent lexical, place-name and syntactic review remains pending.

Complete working English

Ibrahim ibn al-Mundhir related to us. He said: Anas ibn ‘Iyad related to us. He said: Musa ibn ‘Uqbah related to us, from Nafi‘, that ‘Abd Allah informed him:

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to stop at Dhu al-Hulayfah when performing ‘umrah, and on his Hajj when he performed Hajj, under a samurah tree at the site of the mosque at Dhu al-Hulayfah. When he returned from an expedition on that road, or from Hajj or ‘umrah, he descended into the valley bed. When he emerged from the valley bed, he made his camel kneel on the pebbly ground at the eastern edge of the valley and rested there until morning. It was not by the mosque built of stones, nor on the hill on which the mosque stood. There was a channel there, beside which ‘Abd Allah used to pray. In its bed were heaps of sand. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to pray there. Then the flood pushed pebbles into it until it buried the place where ‘Abd Allah used to pray.

And ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Umar related to him that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, prayed where the small mosque stood, before the mosque at Sharaf al-Rawha’. ‘Abd Allah used to mark out the place where the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, had prayed, saying: “There, on your right when you stand in the mosque to pray.” That mosque was on the right edge of the road as you go toward Makkah. Between it and the larger mosque was a stone’s throw, or about that.

And Ibn ‘Umar used to pray toward al-‘Irq at Munsaraf al-Rawha’. The end of al-‘Irq reached the edge of the road, before the mosque between it and al-Munsaraf as you go toward Makkah. A mosque had been built there, but ‘Abd Allah did not pray in that mosque. He left it to his left and behind him, and prayed in front of it toward al-‘Irq itself. ‘Abd Allah used to leave al-Rawha’ and not pray Dhuhr until he reached that place, where he prayed Dhuhr. When coming from Makkah, if he passed it a while before dawn or at the end of the pre-dawn period, he rested until he prayed Subh there.

And ‘Abd Allah related to him that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to stop under a large sarhah tree before al-Ruwaythah, to the right of the road and facing the road, in a broad, level place, until he emerged from a hill two miles before the postal stop at al-Ruwaythah. Its top had broken and bent into its hollow. It remained standing on a trunk, with many heaps of sand at its base.

And ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Umar related to him that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, prayed at the edge of a watercourse beyond al-‘Arj as you go toward a hill. Beside that mosque were two or three graves, with heaps of stones on the graves, to the right of the road by the roadside rocks. ‘Abd Allah used to leave al-‘Arj after the sun had declined in the midday heat and pray Dhuhr in that mosque among those rocks.

And ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Umar related to him that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, stopped by sarhah trees to the left of the road in a watercourse before Harsha. That watercourse adjoined the spur of Harsha, with about a bowshot between it and the road. ‘Abd Allah used to pray toward the sarhah tree nearest the road, which was the tallest of them.

And ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Umar related to him that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to stop in the watercourse at the nearest part of Marr al-Zahran toward Madinah when descending from al-Safrawat. He stopped in the bed of that watercourse, to the left of the road as you go toward Makkah. Between the stopping place of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, and the road was only a stone’s throw.

And ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Umar related to him that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to stop at Dhu Tuwa and spend the night until morning, praying Subh when he came to Makkah. That prayer place of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, was on a rough hill. It was not in the mosque built there, but lower down on a rough hill.

And ‘Abd Allah related to him that the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, faced the two gaps in the mountain between him and the tall mountain toward the Ka‘bah. He placed the mosque built there to the left of the mosque at the edge of the hill. The prayer place of the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, was lower down on the black hill. Leave about ten cubits of the hill, or thereabouts, then pray facing the two gaps in the mountain between you and the Ka‘bah.

Complete working English

‘Abd Allah ibn Yusuf related to us. He said: Malik informed us, from Ibn Shihab, from ‘Ubayd Allah ibn ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Utbah, from ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Abbas, who said:

I came riding a she-donkey when I was nearing puberty. The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, was leading the people in prayer at Mina without facing a wall. I passed in front of part of the row, dismounted, and let the donkey graze. I entered the row, and no one objected to me for that.
