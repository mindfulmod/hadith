Complete working English

Abu al-Yaman related to us. He said: Shu‘ayb informed us, from al-Zuhri, who said: ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn Hurmuz related to me—a client of Banu ‘Abd al-Muttalib (on one occasion he said: a client of Rabi‘ah ibn al-Harith)—from ‘Abdullah ibn Buhaynah, who was from Azd Shanu’ah, was an ally of Banu ‘Abd Manaf and was one of the companions of the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace:

The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, led them in the noon prayer. He stood after the first two rak‘ahs without sitting, and the people stood with him. When he had completed the prayer and the people awaited his closing greeting, he said the takbir while sitting, then made two prostrations before saying the closing greeting. Then he said the closing greeting.

Complete working English

Abu Nu‘aym related to us. He said: Al-A‘mash related to us, from Shaqiq ibn Salamah, who said: ‘Abdullah said:

When we prayed behind the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, we used to say, “Peace be upon Jibril and Mika’il. Peace be upon so-and-so and so-and-so.” The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, turned to us and said, “Allah is Peace. When one of you prays, let him say: ‘Greetings belong to Allah, and prayers and good things. Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and Allah’s mercy and blessings. Peace be upon us and upon Allah’s righteous servants.’ When you say this, it reaches every righteous servant of Allah in heaven and earth. ‘I testify that there is no god but Allah, and I testify that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.’”

Hadith 798

AI-assisted working translation. Independent review pending.

Open this hadith →

Comparison findings

  • All four refuge clauses and the complete final Zuhri–Urwah–Aishah route remain, including informed us in the opening and informed me at the close. The modern print numbers the closing route separately as 833; the selected source and English comparisons keep it with the preceding supplication.
  • The historical print additionally contains a Muhammad ibn Yusuf/Khalaf ibn Amir note about pronouncing the two Masih names, and expands the final Urwah to Ibn al-Zubayr. These are absent from the selected digital text and modern print; they are recorded as witness differences, not silently inserted.

AI-assisted translation of every selected Arabic span, including complete chains, repeated actions, uncertainty, narrator variants and closing routes. Two visually inspected Arabic prints and two individually matched English comparisons were consulted. Independent Arabic, English, chapter, reference and spoken-word review remains pending.

Complete working English

Abu al-Yaman related to us. He said: Shu‘ayb informed us, from al-Zuhri, who said: ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr informed us, from ‘A’ishah, the wife of the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, who informed him:

The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to supplicate in prayer: “O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the punishment of the grave, and I seek refuge in You from the trial of the false messiah, and I seek refuge in You from the trial of life and the trial of death. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from sin and debt.” Someone said to him, “How often you seek refuge from debt!” He said, “When a man falls into debt, he speaks and lies, and he promises and breaks his promise.”

And from al-Zuhri, who said: ‘Urwah informed me that ‘A’ishah, may Allah Most High be pleased with her, said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, seek refuge in his prayer from the trial of the Dajjal.

Complete working English

Qutaybah ibn Sa‘id related to us. He said: Al-Layth related to us, from Yazid ibn Abi Habib, from Abu al-Khayr, from ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Amr, from Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, may Allah Most High be pleased with him:
