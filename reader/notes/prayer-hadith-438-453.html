Comparison findings

  • Both Sufyan once-said variants and the complete closing Ali/Yahya/Abd al-Wahhab, Ja’far and Malik transmissions remain in source order. The historical print places the Malik note before Ali’s note and includes extra wording after Amrah; the modern print follows the selected order and shorter Ali note.   • The selected third-person opening and later first-person I mentioned are retained. Wala’ remains the technical term rather than being reduced to inheritance alone. The who sets free wording follows the Arabic لمن أعتق; the checked EPUB incorrectly says the manumitted. The complete condition statement retains a hundred times, not an inserted a hundred conditions. No legal ruling is supplied outside the source report.

Complete working English

‘Ali ibn ‘Abd Allah related to us. He said: Sufyan related to us, from Yahya, from ‘Amrah, from ‘A’ishah, who said:

Barirah came to her asking for help with her contract of emancipation. She said: “If you wish, I will pay your owners, and the wala’ will belong to me.” Her owners said: “If you wish, you can give her what remains.”

Sufyan once said: “If you wish, you can set her free, and the wala’ will belong to us.”

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, came, I mentioned that to him. He said: “Buy her and set her free, for the wala’ belongs to the one who sets free.” Then the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, stood on the pulpit.

Sufyan once said: “The Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, ascended the pulpit.”

He said: “What is the matter with people who stipulate conditions that are not in the Book of Allah? Whoever stipulates a condition that is not in the Book of Allah has no right to it, even if he stipulates it a hundred times.”

‘Ali said: Yahya and ‘Abd al-Wahhab said, from Yahya, from ‘Amrah. Ja‘far ibn ‘Awn said, from Yahya: “I heard ‘Amrah say: ‘I heard ‘A’ishah.’” Malik related it from Yahya, from ‘Amrah: “That Barirah…” He did not mention “ascended the pulpit.”
