Comparison findings

  • The clothing statement remains explicitly attributed to Umar, separate from the Prophetic answer. All eight stated pairs and the ninth uncertain pair remain in source order. The final I think he said is not converted into a certain ninth instruction. Fath al-Bari explains the past-tense forms as instructions; that is the provisional English rendering here.
  • Source terms are retained to avoid conflating historical garments: izar is a waist wrap, rida an upper wrap, qaba a coat, and tubban a brief lower garment without trouser legs. Shirt renders qamis; trousers renders sarawil. These descriptions are review notes, not inserted into the quotation. Fath al-Bari distinguishes the garments. No otherwise or additional condition from an English comparison is inserted.

Complete working English

Sulayman ibn Harb related to us. He said: Hammad ibn Zayd related to us, from Ayyub, from Muhammad, from Abu Hurayrah, who said:

A man stood up before the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, and asked him about praying in a single garment. He said: “Can all of you find two garments?”

Then a man asked ‘Umar, who said: “When Allah gives you abundance, make use of it. Let a man gather his garments about him. Let a man pray in an izar and a rida, in an izar and a shirt, in an izar and a qaba, in trousers and a rida, in trousers and a shirt, in trousers and a qaba, in a tubban and a qaba, in a tubban and a shirt.” He said: And I think he said: “In a tubban and a rida.”
