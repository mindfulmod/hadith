Why these remain separate

Both printed editions contain the two occurrences at the section’s end. Al-Qastallani discusses the repeated chapter, differences between copies, the reversed order of the reports and a feminine “She said” variant. Similar accounts already appear at app references 833 and 832; their words and occurrences are not interchangeable.

Chapter: Women praying behind men · repeated printed occurrence

Complete narrator chain

Abu Nu‘aym related to us. He said: Ibn ‘Uyaynah related to us, from Ishaq, from Anas, who said:

Report

The Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, prayed in the house of Umm Sulaym. An orphan and I stood behind him, and Umm Sulaym stood behind us.

App reference 835c

Printed reference 875

Dar Ibn Kathir: 875Dar al-Tasil: 885Earlier parallel: app 832

Chapter: Women praying behind men · repeated printed occurrence

Complete narrator chain

Yahya ibn Qaza‘ah related to us. Ibrahim ibn Sa‘d related to us, from al-Zuhri, from Hind bint al-Harith, from Umm Salamah, who said:

Report

When the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said the closing greeting, the women would rise as soon as he finished saying it. He would remain in his place a little while before rising.

Narrator comment

She said: We think—and Allah knows best—that this was so the women could leave before the men caught up with them.

Complete Arabic transcription

حدثنا يحيى بن قزعة حدثنا إبراهيم بن سعد عن الزهري عن هند بنت الحارث عن أم سلمة قالت كان رسول الله ﷺ إذا سلم قام النساء حين يقضي تسليمه وهو يمكث في مقامه يسيرا قبل أن يقوم قالت نرى والله أعلم أن ذلك كان لكي ينصرف النساء قبل أن يدركهن الرجال

Manual unvowelled transcription of the complete printed reports, including every chain word and the Prophet honorific represented as ﷺ. Vowel marks, ornament, report numbers, quotation punctuation, editorial cross-references and footnote markers are outside the reading blocks. Lexical words are retained; no copy is represented as a verbatim facsimile. Independent transcription review remains pending.

Differences that remain visible
  • The first qala after Yahya, the Umm Salamah honorific and the final ahad min in app 832 are absent here. The selected tail has wa-huwa yamkuthu instead of wa-yamkuthu huwa.
  • The closing qalat is feminine: She said is retained without assigning a name. Both inspected prints have this form; al-Qastallani records it and the masculine Ibn Asakir variant.
  • The English comparison names al-Zuhri, while its displayed Arabic says qalat. That attribution is not inserted into this working translation. The plural We think is retained.
